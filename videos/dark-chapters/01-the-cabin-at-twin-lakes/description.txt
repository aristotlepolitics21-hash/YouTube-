In 1968, Dick Proenneke flew into the Alaskan wilderness with a few hand tools. He built a log cabin on the shore of Twin Lakes himself, and then lived there, mostly alone, for about thirty years.

How he did it: logs cut the summer before, notched and stacked by hand, and the whole build filmed on a camera he set up on a tripod. His journals became the book One Man's Wilderness, his film became the documentary Alone in the Wilderness, and the cabin still stands in Lake Clark National Park and Preserve.

Dark Chapters #1 - moments from history, animated in 3D.
