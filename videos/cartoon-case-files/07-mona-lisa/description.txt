The Man Who Stole the Mona Lisa | Animated True Crime

In 1911, the most famous painting in the world vanished from the Louvre, and for a whole day nobody noticed. This is the theft of the Mona Lisa, animated.

The theft: Vincenzo Peruggia, a handyman who had once worked at the Louvre, put on a worker's white smock on a Monday when the museum was closed, took the painting off the wall and walked out with it under his smock
The empty wall: a painter found only four iron hooks the next day, and crowds lined up to stare at the empty space
The suspects: the poet Guillaume Apollinaire was arrested and Pablo Picasso was questioned, and both were innocent
The trunk: the painting sat in a trunk in Peruggia's Paris room for more than two years, until he offered it to a Florence art dealer in 1913
The ending: he served less than a year in prison, and the theft helped make the Mona Lisa the most famous painting in the world

Accounts differ on some details, such as whether he hid in the museum overnight; we've kept to what most sources agree on.
