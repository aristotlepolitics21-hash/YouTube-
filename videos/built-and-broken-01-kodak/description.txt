In 1975, a Kodak engineer built the world's first digital camera. Thirty-seven years later, Kodak filed for bankruptcy. This is how a company can see the future first and still lose to it.

Kodak didn't miss digital. It invented the digital camera, built a portfolio of more than 1,100 digital imaging patents, and was the number one digital camera seller in the US in 2005. But its profits came from film, and every digital camera it sold ate into the business that paid the bills. Fuji cut back its own film business and diversified. Kodak protected the old model until the phone made it irrelevant.

Key facts, checked before production:
- 1975: Steven Sasson's prototype, about 8 lb, 0.01 megapixels, 23 seconds to record a photo
- 1976: Kodak sold about 90% of the film and 85% of the cameras in the US
- 1988: peak employment, 145,300 people
- January 2012: Chapter 11. April 2012: Facebook buys Instagram (13 employees) for about $1 billion
- December 2012: Kodak's digital imaging patents sold for about $525 million. Emerged from bankruptcy in 2013
