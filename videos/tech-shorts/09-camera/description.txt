How Your Phone Camera Takes a Photo

Every photo you take is really several photos, stacked and blended in a split second. Here's what happens when you tap the shutter, animated.

1. Catching light: a tiny stack of lenses focuses light onto a sensor smaller than your fingernail, covered in millions of light buckets called photosites
2. Light into numbers: light knocks electrons loose in each bucket, and the phone turns every bucket into a number, so a photo is a giant grid of numbers
3. Adding colour: each bucket sits under a red, green or blue filter (twice as many green, because our eyes are most sensitive to it), and the phone estimates each pixel's full colour from its neighbours
4. Stacking shots: the phone is often already capturing before you press, blends frames to keep detail in bright skies and dark shadows, and night mode stacks many frames to average away noise
5. Finishing touches: portrait mode estimates depth and blurs the background, then a processor fixes colour, sharpens and compresses the file
