How Your Phone Stores Photos (Flash Memory)

Every photo on your phone is stored as electrons, trapped in tiny cages that hold them even with the power off. Here's how flash memory works, animated.

1. Billions of cells: a photo is a long list of ones and zeros, kept in billions of tiny memory cells; a 256 GB phone holds about two trillion bits
2. Trapping electrons: each cell has an insulated cage called a floating gate; a jolt of voltage pushes electrons through the insulation and into the cage, where they stay without power
3. Reading it back: the chip checks how easily current flows past the cage, and by trapping different amounts of electrons a cell can store three or four bits
4. Stacking up: cells are stacked into towers more than 200 layers high
5. Wearing out: each rewrite wears the insulation a little, so a controller spreads writes evenly (wear levelling) and shuffles data because flash erases in big blocks
