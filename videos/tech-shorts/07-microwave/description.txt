How a Microwave Actually Heats Food

Your microwave doesn't heat your food with heat. It shakes the water inside it. Here's what happens behind the door, animated.

1. The magnetron: turns electricity into microwaves, which a metal channel guides into the box to bounce off the walls
2. Shaking water: water molecules are lopsided, and the waves' electric field flips billions of times a second, so they keep twisting, and that jostling is heat
3. Hot and cold spots: the bouncing waves stack up unevenly, so the plate turns to carry food through the hot spots
4. Outside in: microwaves only reach a few centimetres in, so stir halfway or let food stand; defrost mode pulses the power because ice molecules are locked in place
5. The door and the fork: the door's metal mesh lets light out but keeps microwaves in, and sharp metal can gather enough charge to spark
