In 2000, Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster said no. But that isn't the whole story: Blockbuster later fought back, and for a while it was winning. This is how it lost anyway.

Blockbuster's mistake wasn't missing Netflix. It launched Blockbuster Online in 2004, scrapped late fees in 2005, and in 2006 launched Total Access, which let online customers swap DVDs in its stores. Total Access passed 2 million subscribers and worried Netflix. Then, under financial pressure and after a boardroom fight, Blockbuster cut back on its own answer, just as streaming and $1 Redbox kiosks arrived.

Key facts, checked before production:
- 19 October 1985: David Cook opens the first Blockbuster in Dallas
- 1994: Viacom buys Blockbuster in a deal worth $8.4 billion
- 2000: Blockbuster collects nearly $800 million in late fees, about 16% of revenue
- September 2000: Reed Hastings, Marc Randolph and Barry McCarthy offer Netflix to Blockbuster for $50 million
- 2004 (peak): 9,094 stores, about 84,300 employees, $6.1 billion revenue
- 1 January 2005: Blockbuster ends late fees at company-operated US stores
- 2006: Total Access launches; Netflix reaches 6 million subscribers by year-end
- 2007: John Antioco leaves after a dispute with the board; Jim Keyes becomes CEO. Netflix launches streaming
- 23 September 2010: Blockbuster files for Chapter 11. Netflix ends 2010 with 20 million subscribers
- 2011: Dish wins the auction for Blockbuster's assets with a bid of about $320 million
