In July AD 64, a great fire swept through Rome, and the emperor Nero has been blamed for it ever since. But almost everything we know about him was written by his enemies, decades later. This documentary separates what is well attested from what is rumour.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Lucius Domitius Ahenobarbus on 15 December AD 37 at Antium
- His mother, Agrippina the Younger, married the emperor Claudius in AD 49; Claudius adopted Nero in AD 50
- Became emperor in October AD 54, aged sixteen
- Had his mother, Agrippina, killed in AD 59
- The Great Fire of Rome began on the night of 18–19 July AD 64; Tacitus says three of fourteen districts were destroyed and seven badly damaged
- Tacitus records that Nero blamed and punished Rome's Christians
- Declared an enemy of the state by the Senate and died by suicide on 9 June AD 68, aged thirty
- The main ancient sources (Tacitus, Suetonius, Cassius Dio) wrote between about 50 and 150 years after his death
