# 01 · Adolf Hitler: "They gave him the keys"

**Format:** vertical 9:16 · 60 s · 4 scenes × 15 s · Zack D Films–style hyper-real 3D horror: smooth
grey-skinned characters, cold desaturated grade with one blood-red accent, flickering practical light,
deep shadows, slow creeping push-ins broken by violent edits. Word-by-word captions, centre screen, white
with the key word in red. Fast, low, calm narration, about 40 words per scene. Horror through dread,
sound and implication: no gore, and no victims shown being harmed.

**Edit grammar:** `[RAMP]` speed ramp · `[GLITCH]` 4-frame RGB-split glitch cut · `[FLASH]` 2-frame white
flash · `[PUNCH]` 110% zoom punch on the beat · `[FREEZE]` freeze-frame with red circle · `[SMASH]` smash
cut to silence · `[SHAKE]` camera shake · `[J]` J-cut, next scene's sound starts early.

**Loop:** the last line flows into the first ("…he was given power. / In 1933 a president signed…"),
so the Short replays seamlessly.

---

## Scene 1 · 0:00–0:15 · The signature

**Visual**
- 0:00–0:02 · Pitch black. A single bulb snaps on. Extreme close-up of an old man's trembling hand signing a document. `[PUNCH]` on the pen touching paper.
- 0:02–0:05 · Over his shoulder: a man with a toothbrush moustache watches, face half in shadow. His eyes slowly lift to the lens. `[FREEZE]` red circle on the eyes.
- 0:05–0:10 · The camera dives into the ink on the page `[RAMP]` and out the other side into a bunker, twelve years later. The same man, grey and shaking, alone. The bulb swings; shadows of soldiers stretch across the wall and vanish. `[GLITCH]`
- 0:10–0:15 · Top-down rise through concrete and earth to Berlin burning at night. The camera keeps rising until the city is a red ember in black. `[SHAKE]` on each distant blast.

**On-screen text:** THEY HANDED HIM THE KEYS.

**VO (41 words)**
> In 1933, an old president signed one piece of paper, and handed this man the most powerful country in
> Europe. Legally. No coup. No gunfire. Twelve years later, millions were dead, Berlin was burning, and
> he was hiding underground. Here's how.

**Sound:** silence → bulb click · scratching pen, amplified · sub-bass drop on the eyes · ringing tinnitus over the dive · muffled artillery thuds.

---

## Scene 2 · 0:15–0:30 · The rejected boy

**Visual**
- 0:15–0:18 · A cold, cavernous art school hall. A young man holds out a watercolour. A huge door slams shut in his face, shaking dust from the ceiling. `[PUNCH]` · `[J]`
- 0:18–0:21 · A mirror in a dark hostel room. He stares into it; time-lapse ages the room around him while he doesn't move. His reflection is a beat late. `[GLITCH]`
- 0:21–0:26 · Trench at night. Flares. He sprints through mud with a message. A green fog crawls over the lip of the trench and swallows the frame. `[RAMP]` to slow motion as the fog reaches his face.
- 0:26–0:30 · Hospital ward, bandage over his eyes. A newspaper slides onto the blanket: GERMANY SURRENDERS. His bandaged face slowly turns toward the camera. `[SMASH]`

**On-screen text:** REJECTED. GASSED. HUMILIATED.

**VO (42 words)**
> He wanted to be a painter. Vienna's art academy rejected him. Twice. In the First World War, a gas
> attack left him temporarily blind. He was lying in a hospital bed, eyes bandaged, when Germany surrendered.
> And he wanted someone to blame.

**Sound:** door boom · clock ticking faster under the time-lapse · whistles and boots · gas hiss rising to a scream · dead silence on the smash cut.

---

## Scene 3 · 0:30–0:45 · The crowd

**Visual**
- 0:30–0:33 · Low angle in a smoky beer hall. He grips a lectern and screams; the crowd's faces are lit only by his spotlight, mouths open, all in sync. `[SHAKE]` on every shout.
- 0:33–0:36 · Prison cell. He dictates by lamplight; the typewriter's keys hammer like gunshots. Each keystroke `[FLASH]`es a word on screen: ENEMY. BLAME. EAST.
- 0:36–0:41 · A ballot box pulses like a heart. A 3D red bar grows out of it: 2.6%… 18%… 37%. It stops dead below a glowing 50% line and cracks. `[PUNCH]` on each number.
- 0:41–0:45 · Parliament at night. Fire erupts from the dome window. A hand signs a decree headed ENABLING ACT, and the ink runs like blood down the page. `[GLITCH]` into black.

**On-screen text:** NEVER A MAJORITY. DIDN'T MATTER.

**VO (41 words)**
> He found he could hypnotise a crowd. A failed coup put him in prison, where he wrote Mein Kampf. The
> Depression hit. His party won thirty-seven percent, never a majority. It didn't matter. Within weeks,
> parliament voted its own power away.

**Sound:** crowd roar ducked under VO · typewriter cracks like rifle shots · heartbeat from the ballot box · fire roar → pen scratch → sub-bass hit.

---

## Scene 4 · 0:45–1:00 · The cost

**Visual**
- 0:45–0:48 · A map of Europe on a war table. Red tank arrows spread from the centre like veins. `[RAMP]` fast forward as the red covers everything.
- 0:48–0:52 · A long corridor of empty shoes, thousands of them. The camera tracks slowly down it; the light dims shoe by shoe. No people. `[SMASH]` to silence.
- 0:52–0:56 · Back in the bunker: the bulb flickers, flickers, and dies. In the dark, the faint sound of a single chair scraping back.
- 0:56–1:00 · White text burns into black: 6,000,000. Then, beneath it, the opening shot's pen starts to sign again. `[FLASH]` back to the start for the loop.

**On-screen text:** 6,000,000 · NEVER AGAIN

**VO (41 words)**
> He invaded Poland in 1939. His regime murdered six million Jews. In April 1945, with Soviet troops
> streets away, he killed himself in that bunker. Remember how it started. Not with a war. With a
> signature. Because he was given power.

**Sound:** war drum accelerating · total silence in the shoe corridor · bulb fizz and death · chair scrape · one detuned piano note, then the pen scratch from 0:00 for the loop.

## Scene 1 · 0:00–0:15 · The closed door

**Visual**

- 0:02–0:05 · Two grey-skinned guards in a cold corridor, sweating, a wall clock behind them. One reaches for the handle; his hand stops an inch away and trembles. `[FREEZE]` red circle on the hand.
- 0:05–0:10 · Night. The door creaks open `[RAMP]` to slow motion. The camera creeps into a dark room, past an empty armchair, to a still hand on the rug at the edge of the light. `[GLITCH]`

**On-screen text:** TOO AFRAID TO OPEN THE DOOR.

**VO (42 words)**
> In March 1953, his guards waited outside a closed door. Their master hadn't called all day, and
> they were too frightened to go in. That night, they found him on the floor after a stroke. That fear
> was no accident. Here's how.

**Sound:** silence → clock tick, amplified · two men breathing too close to the mic · sub-bass drop on the frozen hand · door creak stretched into a low drone · wind, and one heartbeat per red dot.

---

## Scene 2 · 0:15–0:30 · The clerk's job

**Visual**
- 0:15–0:18 · A candlelit seminary corridor lined with icons. A boy in a black cassock walks away from camera; behind him the candles go out one by one. `[J]` · `[PUNCH]`
- 0:18–0:21 · A blackboard in an empty classroom. The chalk name JUGHASHVILI smears away by itself; steel letters spelling STALIN slam into place. `[GLITCH]`
- 0:21–0:26 · A cramped office at night. He sits at a small desk stamping papers. The camera pulls back `[RAMP]` to reveal a wall of grey officials' portraits stretching into the dark; one by one, every painted face turns to look at him.
- 0:26–0:30 · Close on his face in lamplight, moustache, eyes in shadow. Slowly he turns to the lens. The lamp dies. `[SMASH]` to black.

**On-screen text:** IT SOUNDED LIKE A CLERK'S JOB.

**VO (42 words)**
> A shoemaker's son from Georgia, trained to be a priest, he chose revolution and a new name: Stalin,
> from the Russian word for steel. In 1922 he became General Secretary. It sounded like a clerk's job.
> It let him appoint officials everywhere.

**Sound:** choir slowed and detuned · candle snuffs, one per beat · steel letters clang · the stamp thuds like a heartbeat, faster and faster · dead silence on the smash cut.

## Scene 3 · 0:30–0:45 · The quotas

**Visual**
- 0:30–0:33 · A sickroom. A frail, grey Lenin writes a note by lamplight; the words TOO RUDE surface in the ink. A drawer slides shut on it. `[PUNCH]` on the drawer.
- 0:33–0:37 · A wheat field at dusk. Grain pours from a silo into a train that pulls away; behind it, a village with doors swinging open and empty bowls on every table. `[RAMP]`
- 0:37–0:41 · A map of the USSR on a desk. Red spreads region by region like veins; a number burns into each region until they add up: 680,000. `[FLASH]` on each number.
- 0:41–0:45 · A desk lamp. A hand signs page after page of typed names; the pages stack into a wall that blots out the light. The lamp flickers and dies. `[GLITCH]` into black.

**On-screen text:** ARRESTS. BY QUOTA.

**VO (40 words)**
> Lenin warned he was too rude. The note was kept quiet. Then famine: the state kept taking grain, and
> historians estimate five to eight million died. In 1937 and 1938, police arrested people to quotas.
> He personally signed execution lists.

**Sound:** pen scratch, amplified · train wheels slowing into a heartbeat · a stamp in strict rhythm, like a metronome · tinnitus, then a sub-bass drop as the lamp dies.

---

## Scene 4 · 0:45–1:00 · The door

**Visual**
- 0:45–0:48 · A watchtower buried in snow. The camera tracks along rows of empty bunks; a counter burns in: 18,000,000. `[RAMP]`
- 0:48–0:52 · His defenders' case: factory chimneys, a victory parade under plain red banners. The camera pushes through the parade and the cheering drains away; the marching coats are empty. `[GLITCH]`
- 0:52–0:56 · A family photograph on a kitchen table. One face in it slowly fades to blank paper. `[SMASH]` to silence.
- 0:56–1:00 · Back in the corridor from 0:00: the line of light under the door. A guard's hand hovers by the handle. `[FLASH]` back to the start for the loop.

**On-screen text:** 18,000,000 THROUGH THE GULAG

**VO (42 words)**
> Around eighteen million people passed through his labour camps. His defenders point to factories
> and victory over Hitler. Many historians answer that his own people paid the price. He built a state
> where even his guards were afraid to open a door.

**Sound:** wind over the camp · a brass band warped and slowing · total silence on the photograph · one detuned piano note, then the clock tick from 0:00 for the loop.

## Scene 1 · 0:00–0:15 · The empty city

**Visual**
- 0:00–0:02 · Dawn on a wide boulevard packed with grey-skinned people, waving. Black boots stamp into frame in perfect step. `[PUNCH]` on the first boot.
- 0:02–0:05 · A young soldier in black, cap pulled low, stops in the road. His face turns slowly to the camera; his eyes catch no light. `[FREEZE]` red circle on the eyes.
- 0:05–0:10 · Time-lapse `[RAMP]`: the street drains of people in seconds. Bicycles tip over, a radio keeps playing on a café table, a hospital bed stands alone in a corridor with its sheet thrown back. `[GLITCH]`

**On-screen text:** A WHOLE CITY. EMPTIED IN HOURS.

**VO (41 words)**
> In April 1975, young soldiers in black marched into Cambodia's capital. Crowds came out to greet them.
> Within hours, they ordered everyone out. Homes, hospitals and schools were emptied. Around two million
> were marched into the countryside. Nobody was allowed back.

**Sound:** crowd cheering → boot stamps take over · cheering cuts out dead on the eyes · sub-bass drop · a radio playing to nobody, detuned · wind, shutters, tinnitus.

---

## Scene 2 · 0:15–0:30 · The gentle teacher

**Visual**
- 0:15–0:18 · A dim classroom. A teacher writes on the blackboard, back to camera. The desks behind him are all empty. He turns slowly and smiles softly. `[J]` · `[PUNCH]`
- 0:18–0:21 · Paris at night, rain running down a café window. His reflection in the glass moves a beat late. `[GLITCH]`

**On-screen text:** A RULER WITHOUT A FACE.

**VO (42 words)**
> The man in charge was a soft-spoken former teacher. Pupils remembered him as gentle. He joined
> communists in Paris, then fled to the jungle. The leaders hid behind one name: Angkar. For a long
> time, most Cambodians didn't know who ruled them.

**Sound:** chalk squeak stretched into a whine · rain and a slowed café accordion · the jungle insects stop all at once · stamp → dead silence.

## Scene 3 · 0:30–0:45 · Year Zero

**Visual**
- 0:30–0:33 · Banknotes blow down an empty street past a bank door hanging open; they curl and crumble to ash in mid-air. `[RAMP]`
- 0:33–0:36 · A clock face. Its hands spin backwards and stop at zero. White text burns in: YEAR ZERO. `[FLASH]`
- 0:36–0:41 · Rice paddies at dusk. Rows of identical figures in black bend over the water, faces hidden. On the dyke, a guard's shadow stretches across all of them. `[SHAKE]`
- 0:41–0:45 · A long communal hall. Dozens of empty tin bowls on one table. A single spoon slides off the edge and falls. `[SMASH]` to silence on impact.

**On-screen text:** TO LOSE YOU IS NO LOSS.

**VO (41 words)**
> His regime abolished money, markets, schools, religion and private property. Outsiders called it
> Year Zero. Families were split up. City people were treated as suspects. Survivors remember a slogan:
> to keep you is no gain. To lose you is no loss.

**Sound:** paper flutter · a clock ticking backwards · a children's song hummed off-key over the paddies · spoon clatter, then nothing.

---

## Scene 4 · 0:45–1:00 · Conscience clear

**Visual**
- 0:45–0:48 · A school corridor at night. Classroom doors swing open one by one onto walls of identity-photo frames, thousands of them, every frame blank. `[PUNCH]` on each door.
- 0:48–0:52 · A map of Cambodia. Red spreads from the capital like veins until nearly one square in four is red. Numbers burn in: 1,500,000 – 2,000,000. `[RAMP]`
- 0:52–0:56 · A bare jungle hut. A frail old man with grey hair sits in a chair. His face turns slowly to camera and he speaks without moving his eyes. `[FREEZE]` red circle on the mouth.

**On-screen text:** NEARLY 1 IN 4 · NEVER TRIED

**VO (42 words)**
> The secret prison S-21 held an estimated twelve to twenty thousand people. Only a handful survived.
> Nearly one Cambodian in four died. Pol Pot never faced a court. Frail and ill, he said: my conscience
> is clear. Remember the morning it began.

**Sound:** footsteps echoing in the corridor · a heartbeat slowing · the old man's breathing, close-mic'd · one detuned piano note, then the crowd cheer from 0:00 for the loop.

## Scene 1 · 0:00–0:15 · The photograph

- 0:05–0:10 · The camera rises over the forest `[RAMP]`, faster and faster, across an ocean, to a palace in Brussels. At a tall window, a white-bearded king in uniform stands with his back to us; his reflection in the glass turns its head before he does. `[GLITCH]`

**On-screen text:** HE NEVER SET FOOT THERE.

**VO (40 words)**
> In May 1904, a missionary photographed a man named Nsala. Sentries working for a rubber company had

> who never set foot there. Here's how.

**Sound:** forest insects → silence → flash pop · a single heartbeat on the empty mat · wind rushing into a palace clock ticking · the seal lands with a sub-bass hit.

---

## Scene 2 · 0:15–0:30 · The cake

**Visual**
- 0:15–0:18 · A grand parliament chamber. Rows of grey men shake their heads in perfect sync. A tall door closes on the king. `[J]` · `[PUNCH]`

**On-screen text:** NOT BELGIUM'S. HIS.

**VO (42 words)**
> Belgium's parliament didn't want colonies, so Leopold found one himself. Publicly, he vowed to bring
> civilisation and fight the slave trade. Privately, he wrote that he wanted a slice of this magnificent

**Sound:** parliament murmur played in reverse · knife through icing, amplified and wrong · pages fluttering into a roar · everything stops on the smash cut.

## Scene 3 · 0:30–0:45 · Red rubber

## Scene 4 · 0:45–1:00 · The furnaces
