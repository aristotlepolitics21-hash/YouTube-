Maximilien Robespierre once argued for abolishing the death penalty. A few years later, he was the best-known face of the Reign of Terror, until the Terror consumed him too. This documentary tells how it happened, and why historians still argue about how much of it was his.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 6 May 1758 in Arras, northern France
- Elected to the Estates-General in 1789 as a deputy of the Third Estate
- Argued in the National Assembly in 1791 for abolishing the death penalty
- Told the Convention on 3 December 1792 that 'Louis must die, so that the country may live'; Louis XVI was executed on 21 January 1793
- Joined the twelve-member Committee of Public Safety on 27 July 1793
- About 17,000 people were officially sentenced to death during the Terror; many more died in prison or without trial
- The Law of 22 Prairial (10 June 1794) removed the right to defence counsel before the Revolutionary Tribunal
- Arrested on 9 Thermidor (27 July 1794) and guillotined on 28 July 1794, aged thirty-six
