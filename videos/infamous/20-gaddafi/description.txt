Muammar Gaddafi seized Libya in a coup at the age of twenty-seven and ruled it for forty-two years. His regime was blamed for the Lockerbie bombing, and it jailed, tortured and killed its critics at home. This documentary tells how a Bedouin boy became the West's most notorious dictator, and how it ended in a drainage pipe outside Sirte.

Key facts in this film:
- Born around 1942 near Sirte, in Italian-ruled Libya
- Overthrew King Idris in a coup on 1 September 1969, aged 27
- Declared Libya a 'Jamahiriya' (state of the masses) in 1977
- Pan Am Flight 103 was bombed over Lockerbie on 21 December 1988, killing 270 people
- A Scottish court convicted Libyan intelligence officer Abdelbaset al-Megrahi in 2001
- Human Rights Watch estimates about 1,200 prisoners were killed at Abu Salim prison in June 1996
- UN Security Council Resolution 1973 authorised action to protect Libyan civilians on 17 March 2011
- Captured and killed near Sirte on 20 October 2011
