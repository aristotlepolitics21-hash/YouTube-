On 11 September 1973, the Chilean armed forces bombed their own presidential palace and overthrew an elected government. The general at their head, Augusto Pinochet, ruled Chile for seventeen years. This documentary tells how he took power, what his regime did to its opponents, and why Chileans are still divided over him.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 25 November 1915 in Valparaíso, Chile
- Appointed army commander by President Salvador Allende on 23 August 1973
- Led the military coup of 11 September 1973; ruled until 11 March 1990
- Chile's official truth commissions recognised more than 40,000 victims, including over 3,000 killed or disappeared
- Orlando Letelier and Ronni Moffitt were killed by a DINA car bomb in Washington on 21 September 1976
- Lost the plebiscite of 5 October 1988, with about 56% voting No
- Arrested in London on a Spanish warrant on 16 October 1998; released on health grounds in March 2000
- Died in Santiago on 10 December 2006, aged 91, without being convicted of any crime
