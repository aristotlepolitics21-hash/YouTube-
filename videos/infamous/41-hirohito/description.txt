Emperor Hirohito reigned over Japan through fourteen years of war that killed many millions across Asia and the Pacific, then reigned for more than forty years after it. Was he a powerless figurehead or a willing war leader? This documentary sets out what is known, and why historians still disagree.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 29 April 1901 in Tokyo, grandson of Emperor Meiji
- Became regent in 1921 and emperor on 25 December 1926; his reign was named Shōwa
- Under the Meiji Constitution of 1889 the emperor was sovereign and supreme commander of the army and navy
- Japan seized Manchuria in 1931 and began full-scale war with China in July 1937
- An imperial conference on 1 December 1941 approved war with the United States, Britain and the Netherlands
- Broke the cabinet's deadlock in August 1945; his surrender broadcast aired on 15 August 1945
- Never charged by the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (1946–1948)
- Became 'the symbol of the State' under the 1947 constitution; died 7 January 1989, aged 87
