King John lost Normandy, quarrelled with the pope, probably had his own nephew murdered, and was forced by rebel barons to agree to Magna Carta in 1215. For eight centuries he has been remembered as one of England's worst kings. This documentary weighs the case against him, and the historians who have tried to defend him.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 24 December 1166, the youngest son of Henry II and Eleanor of Aquitaine
- Plotted against his brother Richard I while Richard was held captive in Germany, 1192 – 1194
- Became king on Richard's death, 6 April 1199
- His nephew Arthur of Brittany disappeared in captivity in 1203
- Normandy fell to Philip II of France in 1204
- England was under a papal interdict from 1208 to 1214
- Agreed to Magna Carta at Runnymede, 15 June 1215; Pope Innocent III annulled it on 24 August 1215
- Died at Newark Castle on the night of 18 – 19 October 1216; Magna Carta was reissued in 1216, 1217 and 1225
