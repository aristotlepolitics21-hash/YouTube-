Lavrentiy Beria ran Stalin's secret police from 1938. Under his command the NKVD shot some 22,000 Polish prisoners at Katyn, deported entire nations and ran the Gulag. After Stalin died, his own colleagues arrested him and had him shot. This documentary tells the story of the most feared man in the Soviet Union.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 29 March 1899 in Abkhazia, Georgia, then part of the Russian Empire
- Became head of the NKVD in November 1938, replacing Nikolai Yezhov
- Proposed the execution of Polish prisoners in a memo approved by the Politburo on 5 March 1940; about 22,000 were killed in the Katyn massacre
- Organised the deportation of the Chechen and Ingush peoples in February 1944
- Oversaw the Soviet atomic bomb project; the first Soviet test took place on 29 August 1949
- Arrested at a Kremlin meeting on 26 June 1953
- Sentenced to death by a special session of the Supreme Court and shot on 23 December 1953
- Russia's Supreme Court refused to rehabilitate him in 2000
