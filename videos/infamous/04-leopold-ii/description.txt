From 1885 to 1908, the Congo Free State was not a Belgian colony. It was the private property of one man, King Leopold II, who never set foot there. This documentary tells how he acquired it, what the rubber trade did to its people, and how a shipping clerk, a consul and a missionary with a camera exposed him.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 9 April 1835 in Brussels; King of the Belgians from 1865 to 1909
- Hired the explorer Henry Morton Stanley, who collected treaties with local chiefs from 1879 to 1884
- The Congo Free State, Leopold's personal possession, was recognised in 1885
- Rubber exports rose from about 100 tonnes in 1890 to about 6,000 tonnes by 1901
- Roger Casement's report on abuses was published by the British government in 1904
- Belgium annexed the Congo on 15 November 1908
- Leopold died at Laeken on 17 December 1909
- The death toll is unknown; estimates of population loss run as high as about 10 million and are disputed
