In July 1995, forces under General Ratko Mladić overran the UN safe area of Srebrenica and killed more than eight thousand Bosniak men and boys. After sixteen years on the run, he was arrested in 2011, and in 2017 a UN tribunal convicted him of genocide. This documentary tells his story, from a career army officer to a convicted war criminal.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 12 March 1942 near Kalinovik, Bosnia
- Became commander of the Bosnian Serb army on 12 May 1992
- The siege of Sarajevo lasted from April 1992 to February 1996; around 11,000 people were killed
- Bosnian Serb forces took more than 200 UN personnel hostage in May–June 1995
- Srebrenica fell on 11 July 1995; more than 8,000 Bosniak men and boys were killed
- Indicted by the ICTY in July 1995, with genocide charges for Srebrenica added in November 1995
- Arrested in Serbia on 26 May 2011 after 16 years as a fugitive
- Convicted by the ICTY on 22 November 2017 of genocide, crimes against humanity and war crimes; sentenced to life
- Conviction and life sentence upheld on appeal by the UN Residual Mechanism on 8 June 2021

Sources: International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia (case IT-09-92, Mladić judgment, 2017), UN International Residual Mechanism for Criminal Tribunals (appeal judgment, 2021), Report of the UN Secretary-General on the fall of Srebrenica (A/54/549, 1999), Srebrenica-Potočari Memorial Centre, Encyclopaedia Britannica.
