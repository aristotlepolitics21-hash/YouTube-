In April 1975, the Khmer Rouge took Cambodia's capital and ordered everyone out. In less than four years, between one and a half and two million people died. This documentary tells how a soft-spoken former schoolteacher, Saloth Sar, became Pol Pot, and what his attempt to remake a society from nothing cost.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Saloth Sar, probably in 1925, in Kampong Thom province, French-ruled Cambodia
- Studied in Paris from 1949, where he joined the French Communist Party
- The Khmer Rouge captured Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975 and emptied the city
- An estimated 1.5–2 million people died under Democratic Kampuchea (1975–1979), roughly a quarter of the population
- At least 12,000 people were held at the S-21 (Tuol Sleng) prison; only a handful survived
- Vietnam invaded on 25 December 1978 and took Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979
- Pol Pot died on 15 April 1998 under house arrest by his own movement, never tried
- The UN-backed Khmer Rouge Tribunal convicted three people, including Nuon Chea and Khieu Samphan for genocide (2018)
