Idi Amin seized power in Uganda in 1971 and ruled for eight years. Abroad he was often treated as a joke; at home, human rights groups estimate that between one hundred thousand and half a million people were killed. This documentary tells how a cook in Britain's colonial army became one of Africa's most notorious dictators.

Key facts in this film:
- Born around 1925 in the West Nile region of Uganda; the exact date is unknown
- Joined the King's African Rifles in 1946; one of the first Ugandans commissioned as an officer, in 1961
- Uganda became independent on 9 October 1962
- Seized power in a coup on 25 January 1971 while Milton Obote was in Singapore
- Ordered the expulsion of Uganda's Asian community in August 1972, with 90 days to leave
- Archbishop Janani Luwum died in custody in February 1977
- Israeli commandos rescued hostages at Entebbe on 3–4 July 1976
- Kampala fell to Tanzanian and Ugandan exile forces on 11 April 1979
- Died in Jeddah, Saudi Arabia, on 16 August 2003, never tried for his crimes
