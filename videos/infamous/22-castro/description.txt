Fidel Castro overthrew a dictator in 1959, survived a US-backed invasion and repeated CIA assassination plots, and ruled Cuba for forty-nine years. Admirers credit him with free healthcare and education; critics remember firing squads, political prisoners and a million exiles. This documentary sets out both sides of one of the twentieth century's most divisive leaders.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 13 August 1926 in Birán, eastern Cuba, son of a wealthy Spanish-born sugar planter
- Led the failed attack on the Moncada Barracks on 26 July 1953
- Batista fled Cuba on 1 January 1959; Castro ruled until 2008
- The Bay of Pigs invasion by about 1,400 CIA-trained exiles was defeated on 17–19 April 1961
- The Cuban Missile Crisis took place in October 1962
- The US Senate's Church Committee (1975) documented at least eight CIA plots to kill him
- About 125,000 Cubans left in the Mariel boatlift of 1980
- Handed power to his brother Raúl in 2006 (formally in 2008); died 25 November 2016, aged 90
