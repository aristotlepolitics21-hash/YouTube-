Reinhard Heydrich ran the Nazi police state, commanded the killing squads in the east and chaired the Wannsee Conference, where the murder of Europe's Jews was coordinated. In 1942 two Czechoslovak parachutists ambushed his car in Prague. This documentary tells how a dismissed naval officer became one of the chief organisers of the Holocaust, and what his death cost the Czech people.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 7 March 1904 in Halle an der Saale, Germany
- Dismissed from the German navy by a court of honour in 1931; joined the SS the same year
- Head of the Gestapo from 1934 and of the Reich Main Security Office from September 1939
- Göring authorised him to prepare a 'total solution of the Jewish question' on 31 July 1941
- Chaired the Wannsee Conference on 20 January 1942; the minutes listed 11 million Jews
- Attacked in Prague by Jozef Gabčík and Jan Kubiš on 27 May 1942; died 4 June 1942
- Lidice destroyed on 10 June 1942; 173 men shot and most of its children murdered
- About 1.7 million Jews were murdered in Operation Reinhard, named after him
