In August 1969, followers of Charles Manson murdered seven people in two nights in Los Angeles, among them the actress Sharon Tate. Manson was not at the house on Cielo Drive, but he was convicted of murder for sending the killers. This documentary tells how a lifelong prisoner built a cult, how detectives finally solved the case, and why the victims, not Manson, deserve to be remembered.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 12 November 1934 in Cincinnati, Ohio
- Released from prison in March 1967, having spent more than half his life in institutions
- Sharon Tate, Jay Sebring, Abigail Folger, Wojciech Frykowski and Steven Parent were murdered at 10050 Cielo Drive on 8–9 August 1969
- Leno and Rosemary LaBianca were murdered on 10 August 1969
- Police announced arrests in the case on 1 December 1969
- Manson, Susan Atkins, Patricia Krenwinkel and Leslie Van Houten were convicted on 25 January 1971
- Death sentences became life terms after California's Supreme Court struck down the death penalty in 1972
- Manson died in custody on 19 November 2017, aged 83, after being denied parole 12 times
