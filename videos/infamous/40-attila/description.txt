Attila ruled the Huns for less than twenty years, but in that time he squeezed thousands of pounds of gold out of Rome, ravaged the Balkans, Gaul and Italy, and became a byword for destruction. This documentary tells his story, and explains how much of it comes from his enemies.

Key facts in this film:
- The Huns arrived in eastern Europe around 370, pushing the Goths into the Roman Empire
- Attila and his brother Bleda inherited the Hunnic kingdom from their uncle Rugila around 434
- The peace of 443 raised the Eastern Roman tribute to 2,100 pounds of gold a year
- Bleda died around 445, according to Roman sources killed by Attila
- The Roman diplomat Priscus visited Attila's court in 449 and left an eyewitness account
- Attila invaded Gaul in 451 and was checked by Aetius and the Visigoths at the Battle of the Catalaunian Plains
- Invaded Italy and destroyed Aquileia in 452, then withdrew after meeting an embassy including Pope Leo I
- Died in 453 after his wedding to Ildico; the Huns were defeated by their former subjects at Nedao in 454

Sources: Priscus of Panium (fragments); Jordanes, Getica (c. 551); Encyclopaedia Britannica; Peter Heather, The Fall of the Roman Empire; Christopher Kelly, Attila the Hun: Barbarian Terror and the Fall of the Roman Empire; E. A. Thompson, The Huns.
