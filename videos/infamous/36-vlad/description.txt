Vlad III of Wallachia ruled a small principality on the edge of the Ottoman Empire in the fifteenth century, and his cruelty terrified his enemies. Four centuries later, Bram Stoker borrowed his name for a vampire. This documentary separates the history from the legend.

Key facts in this film:
- Born around 1431, probably in Sighișoara, Transylvania
- His father, Vlad II, joined the Order of the Dragon and took the name Dracul; 'Dracula' means 'son of Dracul'
- Held as a hostage at the Ottoman court with his brother Radu in the 1440s
- Ruled Wallachia three times: briefly in 1448, from 1456 to 1462, and in 1476
- In a letter of February 1462 to Matthias Corvinus, he counted 23,884 people killed in his Danube campaign
- Night Attack on Mehmed II's camp near Târgoviște, June 1462
- Held in Hungary for about twelve years after 1462
- Killed in battle around the turn of 1476–1477; his burial place is unknown
- Bram Stoker's Dracula was published in 1897
