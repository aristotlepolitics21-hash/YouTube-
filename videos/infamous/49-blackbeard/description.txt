Edward Teach, better known as Blackbeard, was a pirate captain for only about two years, yet he is the most famous pirate in history. This documentary separates the record from the legend: the captured slave ship, the blockade of Charleston, the pardon, and the last fight at Ocracoke.

Key facts in this film:
- Probably born around 1680; his birthplace is uncertain, possibly Bristol, England
- Sailed under pirate captain Benjamin Hornigold out of Nassau, Bahamas, from about 1716
- Captured the French slave ship La Concorde near Martinique in November 1717 and renamed it Queen Anne's Revenge
- Blockaded Charles Town (Charleston), South Carolina, for about a week in May 1718, ransoming hostages for a chest of medicines
- Queen Anne's Revenge ran aground at Beaufort Inlet, North Carolina, in June 1718
- Accepted a royal pardon from North Carolina's governor, Charles Eden, in 1718
- Killed by a Royal Navy force under Lt Robert Maynard at Ocracoke Inlet on 22 November 1718
- The wreck found in 1996 was confirmed as Queen Anne's Revenge by North Carolina in 2011

Sources: North Carolina Department of Natural and Cultural Resources (Queen Anne's Revenge project), Encyclopaedia Britannica, Colin Woodard's The Republic of Pirates, Angus Konstam's Blackbeard: America's Most Notorious Pirate, A General History of the Pyrates (1724).
