Hideki Tojo was Japan's prime minister when it attacked Pearl Harbor in December 1941. After the war he was tried in Tokyo, convicted of waging aggressive war and of permitting atrocities against prisoners, and hanged. This documentary tells his story and asks how much of Japan's war was really his.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 30 December 1884 in Tokyo
- Chief of staff of the Kwantung Army in Manchuria from 1937
- Became minister of war in July 1940; Japan signed the Tripartite Pact on 27 September 1940
- Became prime minister on 18 October 1941
- Japan attacked Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941, killing more than 2,400 Americans
- According to the Tokyo tribunal, about 27% of Western prisoners held by Japan died, against about 4% held by Germany and Italy
- Resigned on 18 July 1944 after the fall of Saipan
- Survived a suicide attempt on 11 September 1945
- Convicted by the International Military Tribunal for the Far East on 12 November 1948 and hanged on 23 December 1948

Sources: Judgment of the International Military Tribunal for the Far East (1948), Encyclopaedia Britannica, National WWII Museum (New Orleans), Herbert P. Bix's Hirohito and the Making of Modern Japan, John W. Dower's Embracing Defeat, Rana Mitter's China's War with Japan.
