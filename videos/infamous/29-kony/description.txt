Joseph Kony's Lord's Resistance Army abducted tens of thousands of children in northern Uganda and turned many of them into soldiers. In 2005 he became one of the first people charged by the International Criminal Court. As of 2025, he has never been caught. This documentary tells the story of the war, its victims, and the long hunt for the man behind it.

Key facts in this film:
- Born around 1961 in Odek, near Gulu, northern Uganda
- Led the Lord's Resistance Army from the late 1980s
- 139 girls abducted from St Mary's College, Aboke, on 10 October 1996; 109 were released the same day
- Nearly two million people in northern Uganda lived in displacement camps by 2005
- ICC arrest warrant issued 8 July 2005 for crimes against humanity and war crimes (charges, not a conviction)
- Human Rights Watch documented more than 800 civilians killed in the LRA's 'Christmas massacres' of 2008–09
- The Kony 2012 film was viewed about 100 million times in under a week
- Former LRA commander Dominic Ongwen was convicted by the ICC in 2021 and sentenced to 25 years
