Mao Zedong founded the People's Republic of China in 1949 and ruled it until his death in 1976. His Great Leap Forward caused the deadliest famine in recorded history, and his Cultural Revolution tore the country apart. This documentary tells his story, why many revile him, and why his portrait still hangs over Tiananmen Square.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 26 December 1893 in Shaoshan, Hunan province
- A delegate at the first congress of the Chinese Communist Party in Shanghai, July 1921
- Fewer than 10,000 of the roughly 86,000 who began the Long March in 1934 reached the northwest
- Proclaimed the People's Republic of China on 1 October 1949
- More than 550,000 people were labelled 'rightists' in the 1957 Anti-Rightist Campaign
- The Great Leap Forward (from 1958) led to a famine with estimated deaths of 15–45 million
- Launched the Cultural Revolution in 1966; it ended with his death in 1976
- Died in Beijing on 9 September 1976; a 1981 party resolution condemned the Cultural Revolution but upheld his overall record
