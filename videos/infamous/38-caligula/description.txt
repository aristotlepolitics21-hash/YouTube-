Caligula ruled Rome for less than four years before officers of his own guard killed him in AD 41. His name became a byword for madness and cruelty. This documentary looks at what he really did, and at why so many of the famous stories may not be true.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Gaius Julius Caesar on 31 August AD 12 at Antium
- Nicknamed 'Caligula' ('Little Boot') by his father's soldiers on the Rhine
- His mother, Agrippina the Elder, and two older brothers died in exile or prison under Tiberius
- Became emperor on Tiberius's death in March AD 37, aged twenty-four
- Fell seriously ill in the autumn of AD 37
- Ordered his statue set up in the Temple in Jerusalem in AD 40; the order was delayed and never carried out
- Killed by officers of the Praetorian Guard led by Cassius Chaerea on 24 January AD 41, aged twenty-eight
- Tacitus's account of his reign is lost; the main surviving narratives are by Suetonius and Cassius Dio
