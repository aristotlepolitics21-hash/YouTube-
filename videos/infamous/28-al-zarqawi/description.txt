Abu Musab al-Zarqawi was a petty criminal from Jordan who became the leader of al-Qaeda in Iraq. His bombings of the UN, of Shia shrines and of hotels in Amman were designed to set Iraqis against each other, and his movement later became ISIS. This documentary tells how he rose, what he did, and why his strategy outlived him.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Ahmad Fadil al-Khalayleh in Zarqa, Jordan, on 30 October 1966
- Jailed in Jordan in 1994; freed in a royal amnesty in 1999
- Named by Colin Powell at the UN Security Council on 5 February 2003; a 2006 US Senate report found Saddam's regime did not harbour him
- UN headquarters in Baghdad bombed on 19 August 2003; 22 killed, including Sérgio Vieira de Mello
- Pledged allegiance to Osama bin Laden in October 2004; his group became al-Qaeda in Iraq
- US reward for him reached $25 million
- Amman hotel bombings of 9 November 2005 killed more than 50 people, mostly Jordanians
- Killed in a US air strike near Baqubah, Iraq, on 7 June 2006
