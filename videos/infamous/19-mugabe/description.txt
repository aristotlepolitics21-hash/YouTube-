In 1980 Robert Mugabe was hailed around the world as the liberator of Zimbabwe. By the time the army pushed him out in 2017, his rule had brought massacre, a collapsed economy and one of the worst hyperinflations ever recorded. This documentary tells how one man came to be remembered as both hero and tyrant.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 21 February 1924 at Kutama Mission, Southern Rhodesia
- Spent about a decade in Rhodesian detention, 1964 – 1974
- Became prime minister at independence on 18 April 1980; executive president from 1987
- The Gukurahundi killings in Matabeleland and the Midlands, 1983 – 1987, are commonly estimated to have killed about 20,000 civilians
- Fast-track land seizures of white-owned commercial farms began in 2000
- Inflation peaked at an estimated 79.6 billion percent a month in November 2008
- Forced to resign on 21 November 2017 after a military takeover, aged 93
- Died in Singapore on 6 September 2019, aged 95
