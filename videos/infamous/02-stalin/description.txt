Joseph Stalin ruled the Soviet Union for almost thirty years. He industrialised a peasant country and led it to victory over Hitler, and he presided over a famine, a terror and a camp system that cost millions of lives. This documentary tells how a cobbler's son from Georgia became one of the most feared rulers in history.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Ioseb Jughashvili on 18 December 1878 in Gori, Georgia, then part of the Russian Empire
- Became General Secretary of the Communist Party in April 1922
- Lenin's note of January 1923 suggested removing him from that post
- The famine of 1932–33 killed an estimated 5–8 million people, including around 4 million in Ukraine
- NKVD records show about 1.5 million arrests and about 680,000 executions in 1937–38
- Around 18 million people passed through the Gulag between 1930 and 1953
- Signed a pact with Nazi Germany on 23 August 1939; Germany invaded the USSR on 22 June 1941
- Died on 5 March 1953 after a stroke; denounced by Khrushchev on 25 February 1956
