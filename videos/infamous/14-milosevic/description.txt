In 1987 one sentence to a crowd in Kosovo turned a grey Communist official into the most powerful man in Serbia. Within four years Yugoslavia was at war. This documentary tells how Slobodan Milošević rose on a wave of nationalism, why he became the first sitting head of state charged by an international war crimes tribunal, and why he was never convicted.

Key facts in this film:
- Born 20 August 1941 in Požarevac, Serbia
- Spoke to Serbs at Kosovo Polje on 24 April 1987: 'No one should dare to beat you'
- Slovenia and Croatia declared independence on 25 June 1991
- The Bosnian war (1992–1995) killed about 100,000 people; the Dayton Agreement was signed on 14 December 1995
- NATO bombed Yugoslavia for 78 days, 24 March to 10 June 1999
- Indicted by the ICTY in May 1999, the first sitting head of state charged by an international war crimes tribunal
- Conceded defeat on 6 October 2000 after mass protests in Belgrade
- Transferred to The Hague on 28 June 2001; trial began 12 February 2002
- Died in custody on 11 March 2006 before a verdict; never convicted
