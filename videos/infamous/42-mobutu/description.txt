Mobutu Sese Seko ruled Congo, which he renamed Zaire, for thirty-two years. Backed by Western governments as a Cold War ally, he became one of the world's richest men while his country sank into poverty. This documentary tells how he took power, how he kept it, and what he left behind.

Key facts in this film:
- Born Joseph-Désiré Mobutu on 14 October 1930 in Lisala, Belgian Congo
- Congo became independent on 30 June 1960, with Patrice Lumumba as prime minister
- Mobutu, then army chief of staff, took power briefly in September 1960; Lumumba was killed in Katanga on 17 January 1961
- Seized power in a coup on 24 November 1965
- Renamed the country Zaire in 1971 and himself Mobutu Sese Seko in 1972
- Hosted the 'Rumble in the Jungle' between Muhammad Ali and George Foreman in Kinshasa, 30 October 1974
- Transparency International (2004) estimated he embezzled up to US$5 billion
- Fled Kinshasa on 16 May 1997 as Laurent-Désiré Kabila's forces advanced; died in Rabat, Morocco, on 7 September 1997
