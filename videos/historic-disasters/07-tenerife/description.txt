On 27 March 1977, two Boeing 747s collided on a fog-covered runway at Los Rodeos airport, Tenerife. A misunderstood radio call, a blocked transmission and a captain under pressure led to the deadliest accident in aviation history. This is how it happened, and how it changed the way pilots talk.

Key facts in this film:
- 583 people were killed on 27 March 1977, the deadliest accident in aviation history
- Both jets had been diverted to Los Rodeos after a bomb at Gran Canaria airport
- The KLM captain began the takeoff without clearance in thick fog
- Two simultaneous radio calls blocked the warning from Pan Am
- Tenerife led to standard phraseology and crew resource management training
