In the 1930s, the southern Great Plains of America turned to dust. Millions of acres of grassland had been ploughed up for wheat; when drought came, the wind lifted the topsoil into storms that buried farms and choked people and animals. On 14 April 1935, Black Sunday, the worst of them rolled across the Plains. This is how a man-made disaster grew out of a boom.

- Around 2.5 million people left the Plains states by 1940
