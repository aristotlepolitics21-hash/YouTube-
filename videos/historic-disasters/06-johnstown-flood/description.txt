On 31 May 1889, the South Fork Dam above Johnstown, Pennsylvania, gave way, sending a wall of water and debris into the town below. The dam belonged to a private club of some of the richest men in America, and it had been repaired cheaply for years. This is the story of the Johnstown Flood.

Key facts in this film:
- The South Fork Dam failed at about 3:10 PM on 31 May 1889
- 2,209 people were killed, including 99 entire families
- The South Fork Fishing and Hunting Club had lowered the dam and removed its discharge pipes
- Clara Barton led the American Red Cross in its first major disaster relief effort
- No member of the club was ever held legally responsible
