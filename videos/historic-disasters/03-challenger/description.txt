On 28 January 1986, the space shuttle Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch, killing all seven crew, including teacher Christa McAuliffe. Engineers had warned the night before that cold weather could make the rocket's O-ring seals fail. They were overruled. This is the story of that decision.

Key facts in this film:
- Challenger broke apart 73 seconds after launch on 28 January 1986
- All seven crew were killed, including teacher Christa McAuliffe
- Engineers at Morton Thiokol recommended against launching below 53 °F
- A cold, stiff O-ring in the right solid rocket booster failed to seal
- The Rogers Commission found the decision to launch was flawed
