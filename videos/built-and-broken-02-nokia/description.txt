At the end of 2007, Nokia sold 4 in every 10 phones on Earth. Six years later, it sold its entire phone business to Microsoft. This is how the best phone maker in the world lost the smartphone era.

Nokia didn't miss the smartphone. It launched the 9000 Communicator in 1996, reportedly shelved a touchscreen internet phone around 2004, and in 2007 its Symbian software ran on roughly 6 in 10 smartphones. But Nokia kept score by phones shipped, while Apple and Google changed the game to apps, developers and software. By late 2012 Nokia had 3.5% of the smartphone market.

Key facts, checked before production:
- 1865: Fredrik Idestam opens a pulp mill in Tampere; the company takes the name Nokia in 1871
- 1967: merger with Finnish Rubber Works and Finnish Cable Works
- 1989: Mikhail Gorbachev photographed in Helsinki calling Moscow on a Mobira Cityman
- 1991: Ericsson holds talks to buy Nokia; the deal fails over Nokia's TV business. First GSM call made in Finland on a Nokia phone (1 July)
- 1992: Jorma Ollila becomes CEO and focuses Nokia on telecoms
- 2000: market value briefly about €303 billion; Nokia is about 4% of Finland's GDP and 21% of its exports
- Q4 2007: 40% of global phone sales (133.5 million handsets)
- February 2011: "burning platform" memo; switch to Windows Phone; shares fall 14% on 11 February
- September 2013: Microsoft agrees to buy Nokia's devices business for €5.44 billion
- July 2015: Microsoft writes down $7.6 billion and announces 7,800 job cuts
